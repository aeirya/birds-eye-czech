FOCUS ORDER

Do not spend your first week fighting ř.

Prioritise vowel length, c / č, j, ch / h, and clean unreduced vowels. Then train r / ř and palatal consonants.

SIDE-BY-SIDE CONTRASTS

Train the distinction, not isolated words

Short vs long vowel

byt “apartment” vs být “to be”

The acute accent changes duration, not stress position.

Short vs long vowel

pan “Mr.” vs pán “gentleman/master”

r vs ř

rada “advice” vs řada “row/series”

ř is a fricated trill, not r followed by ž.

š vs ž

šít “to sew” vs žít “to live”

Same place of articulation; ž is voiced.

i vs y spelling

bít “to hit” vs být “to be”

In standard Czech these are homophones: the spelling difference is grammatical/historical.

Final devoicing

led “ice” vs written let

Word-final d is normally pronounced [t], so the surface sounds can coincide.
